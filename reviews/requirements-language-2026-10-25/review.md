# Overview and requirement language review

Reviewed on 25 October 2026 after the owner found C-000 unreadable in Syspresent.

The earlier audit retained C-000 because it explicitly incorporated other
challenge rules. That did not establish whether its main sentence told a reader
what the boat had to achieve. Its wording should have failed the separate
readability review. Correct links and a complete member list were insufficient
reasons to approve the description.

## Scope

This pass covers 34 boat, mission, function, equipment and requirement
descriptions, repeated wording in 176 requirement-link explanations, all 31 Blue Dog story scenes,
the requirements introduction, modeling conventions and current assessment.
Detailed analysis assumptions and every remaining requirement sentence have not
received a new engineering review. This is a scoped correction, not a new
complete-model audit verdict.

C-000 retains its full rule list in named qualification conditions. Descriptions
identify a job, an engineering reason or a specific measurement still needed.
Element identities, relationship types, numerical criteria, mission applicability,
qualification rules and open hardware choices remain in place. Relationship
semantics are explained in the modeling guide.

## Basis and validation

[NASA's requirement-writing checklist](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/appendix-c-how-to-write-a-good-requirement/)
calls for clear, concise statements, one thought per requirement, completeness
and verifiable meaning. The project's reading check asks whether a reader can
identify the required boat outcome before following its references.

Model edits used the checkout's pinned OpenSysML editor and native queries,
`develop-23ca2e2f0`. The connected MCP server identified a different Windows
checkout and runtime `develop-62b904178`; its results were not used to validate
these edits.
