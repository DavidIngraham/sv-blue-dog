# Verification, validation and evidence

Make the claim, planned assessment and actual supporting evidence distinguishable and traceable. Read [the sourced checklist](references/checklist.md) and [source register](references/sources.md). Use native model relationships, verification cases and verdicts.

## Workflow

1. Establish applicable requirements and stakeholder expectations, configuration, operating envelope and intended decision. Inventory verification objectives, method declarations, procedures, execution records, evidence artifacts and acceptance dispositions natively. Separate current mission, future scope and study objectives.
2. Review the argument from claim to criterion, procedure, test article/model, observations, reduction, result and assessment. For validation, include intended use, actual users and operational environment. A covered requirement is not necessarily an executable or accepted claim.
3. Record findings by native identity and checklist/source ID. Identify whether the defect concerns the plan, execution, applicability, measurement, provenance, interpretation or acceptance. Inspect the actual conditions rather than infer sufficiency from the existence of a verify link.
4. Correct authorized plans, method metadata, configuration/analysis bindings, criteria and evidence references. Preserve immutable runs and their original subjects. New results need new provenance; do not relabel historical data as the current installation. Reuse native verdict kinds and calculations.
5. Validate missing/mismatched evidence, failed criteria, stale provenance, boundary uncertainty and incomplete conditions. Keep failed and inconclusive observations visible. Test that removal of a material evidence precondition is observable. Run appropriate regressions and publication checks.
6. Report separately: objectives defined; procedures ready; assessments executed; applicability established; accepted claims. Update the model and current evidence assessment, including unresolved decisions and the next useful measurement.

## Interpretation boundaries

Verification addresses specified requirements; validation addresses stakeholder needs in intended use. The same experiment can support both when each claim and its acceptance basis are explicit. Analysis, inspection, demonstration and test are methods, not maturity levels.

An executable predicate assesses supplied inputs; it does not authenticate observations, calibrate instruments or review evidence automatically. A readiness Boolean is a precondition for a decision, not the decision. A failed test can still be valid evidence. Missing evidence cannot become a pass; a calculation executing successfully does not prove feasibility.

Keep numerical verification, model validation, product qualification and unit acceptance distinct. Evidence from a component, fixture, simulation or another configuration needs a justified transfer argument. Do not assume more printed digits, more samples or zero observed failures establish adequate confidence. Preserve censored/incomplete observations and the denominator/exposure definition.

Tailor procedures to the claim and consequence. Do not impose agency review boards or blanket approval requirements on reversible model work. Physical trial authorization and claim acceptance follow the project's actual responsibilities.
